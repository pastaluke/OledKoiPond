## The pivot

Getting the new creature class **as data/code** is make-or-break for whether this
game becomes all it can be. Rather than committing to the architecture and data
structure up front, **build a list of features the structure must support, and
derive the structure from the list.** If the list gets too big or contradictory,
scope it down. Simple definitions can be made up front; it's okay if they change
later.

## Movement — make the settings make sense

- Move the current "Movement" panel settings **into creature-specific settings**.
- **No pond-wide water resistance** — it's already calculated per-entity; users
  customizing a creature choose how much resistance makes sense for it.
- **Glide depth needs a better explanation.** It felt like it was trying to achieve
  what water resistance does, but its description references an entity's intent to
  maintain a speed while coasting.
- **Burst ate all the functionality** into one method, and because of that we're
  (1) keeping things simple in a user-friendly sense, but (2) making users
  compensate for unrealistic/non-organic movement by tweaking other parameters —
  burst is the only way a fish can emulate just swimming around.

### Real-fish observations (ponds + large tanks, small fish)

- They spend a lot of time at **low speeds**.
- They **don't need to bend their body to rotate** — body-bend steering only
  matters when moving fast enough that the bent shape actually steers them
  through a viscous medium.
- They use **side- and bottom-fins for precise, small movements** to face where
  they want to go, or the food they want to catch.
- They are **not boats** — not limited to "go straight + rudder steer."
- **Below a speed threshold, fish can push themselves in any direction** by
  flapping the appropriate fins — comparable to astronaut RCS thruster packs
  firing in cardinal directions relative to the wearer.

### Move styles (per-creature, not per-pond)

- More than one movement style per creature; styles are creature-specific.
- **Coast is a sub-state of a movement style**, not its own style ("coast" and
  "glide" as separate concepts is too ambitious/redundant). Styles contain simple
  loops that cycle into coasting once a style-specific speed is reached.
- **Movement algorithms could themselves be cartridges** users choose or start
  from: a schooling-oriented one (leveraging neighbor connections to simplify
  group movement), and an individual-oriented one (micro omni-directional
  movement, made realistic by assigning "fins" attributes that move within a
  range given the acceleration direction — simple ranges for fins driving
  forward, deliberate slowing, backwards, directly sideways).

Style ideas:
- **Checking out a nearby stationary/slow object/entity** — shares base
  maneuvering code with "feeding on food within perception radius."
- **Feeding etiquette:** when an entity eats, it starts a short timer. Fish with
  a shorter/no timer are allowed to eat first. A fish holding a timer that
  perceives another fish with no/shorter timer will notice and turn toward food
  without approaching it; with two or more timers running, it ignores the food
  altogether if `hasSmallerTimer` is true on a perceived entity.
- **"Flowing":** the fish desires to keep moving within a speed range, lazily
  accelerates with low-frequency/velocity tail wags mixed with moderate fin
  freq/velocity, looping, letting the organically tuned wander target choose its
  flow direction.
- **Greeting / generic interaction with another creature.**
- **Being part of a large school** — needs cost-effective schooling at larger
  counts. The sim caps out around 40 fish today and can chug there.

### The wag-speed fix

Users need to set the **speed multiplier for tail wag after the pivot point**.
Today wag frequency seems to scale with acceleration intensity, and with burst as
the only movement type you effectively see one frequency — only amplitude is
meaningfully adjustable. Fix: more movement styles + a **wag speed (frequency
multiplier)** control, with a **dedicated preview** showing the entity
transitioning between available move styles, accelerating from slowest to the
highest asked threshold for that style (e.g. burst vs. a purposeful "active
coast/glide").

## Shaders — per-creature + scene cartridges

- Creatures can take on **different, individual shaders** — done clean and low
  cost, optimizing and reusing across entities via smart resource management.
- **Ingest shaders from https://shaders.paper.design** (license permits — verified
  Apache 2.0, `@paper-design/shaders`). Their **water shader** is beautiful; learn
  from it to improve/replace/offer another option for our water.
- A core hook of the game: **a built-in shader library like cartridges** you pick
  to render your scene. Examples:
  - **N64 Zelda fish pond** — low-res textures, baked lighting, cast shape
    shadows, simple entity shadows.
  - **Frutiger Aero** — glass fish in gorgeous A30-beach-style water + sand floor.
  - **Game Boy Color** — GBC resolution + emulated display tech,
    pixel-transparency shaders, hardware-accurate dithering.
  - **Retro terminal** — old-monitor rendering (à la Windows terminal effects),
    monochromatic red/green shades.
- All as light as convincingly possible.

## Depth layers

- Depth is faked by applying a **specifically colored transparency layer** over
  entities at different "levels"; the level determines draw order.
- Every entity gets a **"depth filter"** with color and opacity values.
- **Caustic info is mapped into each layer filter**; per layer, choose whether
  caustics show up exclusively on entities of that plane, and at what opacity
  (separate from the filter's color/opacity).
- Default: a single linear step of very-high-opacity black masks applied to
  entities on layers top to bottom.
- **Users define how many layers there are.** (Just one? Fish overlap randomly
  now — is that helping optimization? Worth studying whether to optimize the
  current process and build more at-scale-friendly entity movement algorithms.)
- **Locking an entity to a layer** — e.g. snails live on the bottom layer / pond
  floor.
- **Food pellets** (planned) translate through layers by **lerping between layer
  filters**, then sit on the bottom layer for a time and dissolve/fade — if the
  food has "sinking" selected. The **layer-filter lerp is also how creatures move
  between layers.**
- Movement changes due to layers are just layer order + filter composition. Fish
  already do a good enough job not clipping into each other; layer height does
  not need to factor into collision avoidance (tunable as-is; may revisit 🤷).

## Pond
