## Verdict on the v1 cartridges

The shipped cartridges (terminal / GBC / paper-water) are visibly different, but
they read as guesses at the intended look, executed by machine learning. They
don't encapsulate how the app is meant to be **experienced / used / customized**.

If cartridges continue as a concept, they must become something users get
**in-game knobs** for — so users can *make, save, and share their own*, not just
pick from prebuilt looks.

## The alternative direction being pondered

Rather than a global "scene shader picker," customization might flow through
**in-fiction actions** — the *world* is the interface:

- A user who wants a "Retro Computer Code Console" pond builds it by **creating
  fish food** that gives fish certain colors and/or applies shaders to the fish
  that eat it.
- The water's look changes through something done **to the water** — maybe a
  cartridge, maybe "**treating**" the pond water somehow.
- Maybe **placing specific objects** (plants, decorations) causes specific
  effects.

The through-line: creation and sharing happen through pond-native verbs (feed,
treat, decorate), and a "setup" someone shares is the recipe of those actions —
not a shader dropdown.

## Status

Unresolved — "I don't know if it's really even the way I want to go… I can
ponder on that more." Nothing here supersedes shipped work; E14-7 stays live as
a v1. When this direction firms up, it likely reshapes E14-7's successor work,
the E12 food epic (food as the shader-delivery vehicle), and E6 (sharing).
